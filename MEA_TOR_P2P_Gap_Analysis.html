MEA P2P TOR: requirements and prototype gap analysis

Source: TOR draft 01 July 2026 (e-bidding DT7-4616-TGZ, 91 pages, RPA removed) · Compared with: Fisko Procurement (P1–P17) and Fisko Finance (F1–F3), build at commit b96cf0d · Prepared 18 Oct 2026

1. Headline

The TOR asks for a P2P system for MEA (การไฟฟ้านครหลวง): procurement management plus payment, integrated with SAP S/4HANA (PS, PM, CO, BU, MM, FI), bundled with a commercial ECM, a no-code workflow engine and an RDBMS, delivered in 540 days. The prototype is a procurement case-management layer designed for a budget agency: it is strong on the case lifecycle, rules, committees, contracts and analytics, and weak or absent on everything that makes this TOR a P2P and a platform bid.

  • Biggest mismatch: the prototype assumes the Thai government chain (Budget → e-GP → GFMIS, file based). The TOR's money side is SAP (AP posting, payment voucher, bank e-payment, WHT, VAT). Fisko Finance reconciles GFMIS files and has none of the SAP-side functions.
  • Three whole TOR packages have no prototype counterpart: ECM (TOR 5.3), workflow and form designer (5.4), and SAP integration (5.2).
  • Real functional gaps inside Procurement: vendor-facing role, sample and type-test workflow, drawing approval, serial numbers, committee fees, bid security, appeal forwarding to the Comptroller General's Department (อธ.1), RFQ, e-mail delivery and e-signature.
  • Good news: a large share of the Annex 12 detail (plan versions, TOR library, committees and conflict of interest, evaluation scoring, appeal window, contract guarantee, penalties, warranty claims, supplier register, สขร.1 report, dashboards, audit dossier) is already demonstrable.

3. Recommended order of work

  1. Decide the money-side stance first. Either rename Finance to a P2P "AP and payment" app with a SAP posting layer (simulated SAP interface, invoice posting, payment voucher, bank e-payment advice, WHT and VAT), or keep GFMIS for government customers and add a switchable "SAP mode". Everything under 4.8–4.11 depends on this.
  2. Close the Procurement functional gaps that are cheap and demoable: vendor portal role (RFQ reply, price list, invoice upload, bank-account change form), RFQ and invitation letters with e-mail simulation, sample and type-test tracking, drawing approval, bid security, appeal forwarding (อธ.1) with reminders, committee fee calculation, Serial Number capture, SME and MiT tracking in evaluation.
  3. Add the three platform packages as design pages, then thin demos: ECM (folder, metadata form, version, full-text search, watermark view, retention), a form and workflow designer (drag and drop, multi-position, out-of-office, SLA, e-mail approval), and an integration console (SAP, COI system, GDX, ThaID, PDPA consent, SMTP, Smart Office).
  4. Write the non-functional answers for the bid: 3-tier on MEA Cloud sizing, 50 transactions a minute, TLS 1.2, 90-day logs, backup, OWASP, Gartner-leader AST.

4. Open questions for the customer or bid team

  • Does MEA follow the Government Procurement Act 2560 methods exactly (e-bidding, selective, specific, small-budget under ministry rules)? The TOR says yes (3 workflow diagrams in Annex 13). The prototype's method rules are placeholders to be confirmed.
  • Is e-GP integrated by API or by file? The TOR only says "send/receive via API, Batch or Web Service as MEA specifies".
  • The TOR points to Annex 12 as an "example overview" and Annexes 1, 2 and 6 are blank placeholders. Which parts of Annex 12 are binding requirements?
  • Marked as removed or struck through in the draft (5.2.5.1, 5.2.6.2, 5.2.6.3, 5.4.7): confirm they are out of scope.
  • Training: at least 245 users, 10 + 10 admin, 10 + 10 developer seats on ECM and workflow. Is Fisko in the 245?

5. Reading notes and limits

  • The PDF has no usable Thai text layer, so every page was read as an image. Pages 35–38 (maintenance, SLA, change-request terms), 53–55 (licence and warranty terms) were read for scope only; they hold no functional requirements for the prototype.
  • Requirement numbering in the TOR is inconsistent (for example 5.1.12.x under 5.1.13 and 5.2.6.7 used twice). References below use the TOR's printed numbers.
  • Highlighted passages in the TOR (cyan, yellow, magenta) mark late edits: the 50-transactions-per-minute performance test (3.4.5), the COI reporting interface (4.10.2), DOPA ThaID (4.10.11), PR/PO send and receive (5.1.12.7–8), bid-security and contract-guarantee process (5.1.16, red), log monitoring (5.4.32), and the Web designer for integrations (5.4.40.1).
  • Status is judged from the code in src/js/6*.js and the design documents, not from a full click-through of every screen.
